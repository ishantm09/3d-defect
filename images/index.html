About this project

What is this project?

A student-built web tool that spots common 3D-printing failures from a single photo or from a live camera view. It looks at a picture of a print, marks where the problem is, names the defect, shows how confident it is and suggests what to do next.

Why did we build it?

A failed print wastes filament, hours of machine time and often the whole part. Failures such as spaghetti usually start small and are easy to stop early, but nobody can watch a printer for hours. This project explores whether a camera and a small AI model can do that watching.

What it can detect

  • Spaghetti: the print has come loose and filament is piling up in a tangle.
  • Stringing: thin threads left between separate parts of the print.
  • Warping: corners lifting off the bed as the part cools.
  • Cracking: fine splits between layers, usually caused by weak layer bonding.
  • Under-extrusion: too little filament comes out, leaving gaps or thin, weak layers.
  • Layer shifting: layers slip sideways, so the part looks skewed.
  • Normal: no defect found.

How it works

1
Take or upload a photo
or start Live Monitor with a camera pointed at the printer.
2
An AI model examines the image
an object-detection model (YOLOv8) running right in your browser, so your photos stay on your device.
3
You get the result
defect name, confidence, a box around the problem and practical advice.
4
Live Monitor keeps watch
it checks every 10 to 30 seconds and raises an alert if a defect shows up in 3 checks in a row.
Detection accuracy differs from one defect type to another, and results can be wrong. Please confirm important results with a manual inspection.
